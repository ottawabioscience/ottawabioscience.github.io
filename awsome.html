Demystify the Black Box

Advanced science in community settings through immersive biotechnology workshops and accessible scientific literacy.

The Project

Deliver a series of 7 immersive community workshops and build a culture of accessible scientific literacy. This initiative aims to help protect communities against misinformation and pseudoscience that increasingly shape public discourse and social division.

Most scientific progress stays trapped in academic journals or high-end laboratories, never reaching ordinary citizens. This information gap allows fear and misinformation to take root.

The only way to truly build public trust in science is through direct hands-on experience. By putting the tools of genetic engineering directly into people's hands, we transform abstract scientific concepts into tangible reality.

Why This Matters

Biotechnology is often seen as mysterious and inaccessible. Our workshops remove the jargon and demonstrate that genetic engineering is a logical, understandable tool that can be explored in any community setting.

In today's world, many people understand smartphones better than they understand their own biology. While digital technology surrounds us, the biological code that defines life remains hidden from public understanding.

This project will take science out of the ivory tower and place it directly into the public square where everyone can participate.

Concrete Implementation

Golden Gate Assembly

Imagine DNA as LEGO bricks. Golden Gate Assembly uses precise molecular “scissors” that allow multiple DNA pieces to connect perfectly together in a single reaction, creating seamless genetic designs without the scars left by older techniques.

Cellular Reagents

Instead of relying on fragile purified enzymes and expensive laboratory freezers, we use living bacterial packets that contain the tools needed for molecular biology reactions. This makes advanced science portable, affordable, and suitable for libraries, museums, and community centers.

Coral Chromoproteins

Participants will clone colorful coral DNA sequences that produce vibrant pink, teal, and purple proteins. Successful cloning experiments create visible color changes in bacteria, providing instant visual proof of scientific discovery.

Coral Chromoproteins

Chromoproteins are naturally occurring pigment proteins found in marine organisms such as corals. Unlike fluorescent proteins, they produce strong visible coloration through light absorption and reflection, resulting in vivid pinks, purples, teals, and deep blues.

In biotechnology workshops, these proteins act as a visual reporter system: successful genetic modification is confirmed when bacterial colonies visibly change color. This provides an immediate and intuitive bridge between DNA manipulation and real-world biological outcomes.

By making molecular biology visible, chromoproteins transform genetic engineering from an abstract concept into a tangible, observable process accessible to community learners.

Building Scientific Literacy

By teaching people how to “read and write” DNA, we replace fear with evidence-based curiosity. Our goal is to strengthen the civic fabric of Ottawa by empowering citizens to think like scientists through direct participation and discovery.

We believe science belongs to everyone — not just experts, institutions, or corporations. Through open learning and public participation, Ottawa can become a pioneer in accessible biotechnology education.
